# Standing Desk

#health #ergonomics

## Why I switched

By late 2024 I was sitting for something like 11 hours a day: about 8 at work, then another 2 to 3 in the evening on master's coursework. My lower back had gone from "a bit stiff on Monday mornings" to "can't put my socks on without bracing against the wall." The flare-ups are all in the [[Back Pain Log]]. The worst week, early December, hit a 7/10 after a release crunch where I barely got up from my chair.

What finally pushed me was the [[Project Retrospective]] in January. Half the team admitted the crunch had wrecked their sleep, their backs, or both. One of the action items was literally "people should take breaks." I decided to stop treating my desk setup as something to fix "later."

Other reasons:
- The physio said that changing position often matters more than any one "perfect" posture
- I work from home 3 days a week, so the home office is where most of the damage happens
- My employer pays up to 400 EUR a year toward home office ergonomics, and I hadn't used it in 2024 either

## The setup

Bought 2025-01-11, set up 2025-01-12 (took 2 hours, mostly because of cable management).

- **Frame:** electric dual-motor frame, height range 62 to 127 cm, 4 memory presets. 389 EUR
- **Top:** 160 x 80 cm bamboo top, 89 EUR. The 140 cm one was too narrow for two monitors plus the notebook I use for thesis notes
- **Anti-fatigue mat:** 45 EUR. Turned out to be non-negotiable, my feet hurt after 20 minutes on bare parquet
- **Monitor arm:** dual arm, 72 EUR, so I can get the screens to eye height in both positions
- **Cable tray + spine:** 25 EUR
- **Total:** 620 EUR, 400 EUR of it reimbursed by work (claim approved 2025-01-29)

My heights (I'm 183 cm):
- Sitting preset: 73 cm, elbows at 90 degrees
- Standing preset: 112 cm. I started at 115 and my shoulders crept up, so I lowered it
- Top of monitor at roughly eye level in both positions

## Sit/stand schedule

What I tried first (week 1) was standing for 2 hours straight in the morning. Bad idea. My calves were sore and I was leaning on one hip by the end of it, which is arguably worse than sitting.

The schedule I ended up with, from week 3 on:

| Time | Position | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 to 09:15 | Standing | Email, Slack, standup |
| 09:15 to 11:00 | Sitting | Deep focus coding block |
| 11:00 to 11:45 | Standing | Code review, PR comments |
| 11:45 to 12:30 | Sitting | |
| 12:30 to 13:15 | Lunch, walk outside | Away from the desk |
| 13:15 to 14:00 | Standing | Meetings, camera on |
| 14:00 to 15:30 | Sitting | Second focus block |
| 15:30 to 16:00 | Standing | Admin, ticket grooming |
| 16:00 to 17:30 | Alternating every 30 min | Timer on phone |

Rules I keep:
- Roughly 30 to 40 percent of the day standing, never more than 45 minutes at a time
- Every meeting where I don't need to type, I stand
- Every position change also means 10 squats or a short hip flexor stretch
- On the 2 office days, I book a desk with a height-adjustable frame (there are 6 on our floor, so book them by Monday)
- Evenings for thesis work: start standing for the first 30 minutes, then sit

The timer matters. Without it I sit down at 09:15 and look up at 12:40.

## Log of how it went

- **Week 1 (Jan 13 to 19):** sore calves, sore feet before the mat arrived. Back pain unchanged around 5/10
- **Week 2:** mat arrived. Standing time up to about 2.5 hours a day. Back is maybe slightly better in the afternoons
- **Week 3:** switched to the schedule above. First week without waking up stiff on more than 2 days
- **Week 5 (mid-February):** realised I'd stopped taking ibuprofen in the evenings. Pain mostly 3/10
- **Week 8 (early March):** one bad day (5/10) after a 4-hour planning workshop sitting on a bad office chair, otherwise 2 to 3
- **Week 11:** standing feels normal now. Calves fine. I notice when I've sat too long, which I never did before

## Things that didn't work

- Typing while standing for long stretches. My wrists complain, so deep coding stays seated
- A balance board I borrowed from a colleague. Fun for 10 minutes, distracting after that
- Standing during 1:1 video calls where I take notes. Too much shifting, so I sit for those now

## Conclusion, March 2025

Worth it. After about 11 weeks:
- Average back pain went from roughly 5/10 in December to 2 to 3/10 in March (see [[Back Pain Log]])
- Ibuprofen use dropped from around 4 evenings a week to maybe once every two weeks
- Afternoon energy is noticeably better, fewer 15:00 slumps
- Real cost after the reimbursement: 220 EUR. Cheapest health improvement I've made in years

The desk itself isn't magic. What helped was moving more often, and the desk makes that easy. If I had to redo it, I'd buy the mat and the monitor arm on day one and skip the balance board.
