## The headline: HbA1c 7.2%

HbA1c is the average blood sugar over roughly the last 2 to 3 months.
- Below 5.7%: normal
- 5.7 to 6.4%: prediabetes range
- 6.5% and above: diabetes range

Mine came back at **7.2%** (55 mmol/mol). That's not borderline. It's clearly in the diabetes range.

Fasting glucose was also high at **138 mg/dL** (7.7 mmol/L). The cutoff for diabetes is 126 mg/dL on repeated tests.

Honestly, I didn't see this coming. I'm 36, I run 3 times a week, and I lost almost 3 kg during the [[Intermittent Fasting]] experiment in spring. I thought I was the "healthy one" this year.

## What the doctor said (2025-10-14)

Summary from my notes during and right after the appointment:

- **One test isn't a diagnosis.** HbA1c of 7.2% plus fasting glucose of 138 together are "very suggestive" of type 2 diabetes, but she wants a **repeat HbA1c and fasting glucose** to confirm. Not a lab error, she said, but that's the protocol
- **No medication yet.** If it's confirmed, she'd probably start metformin, but she wants to see what 3 months of lifestyle changes do first, given my age and that I'm already active
- **Weight:** losing 5 to 7 percent of body weight (so 4 to 6 kg) would make a real difference to insulin sensitivity
- **Food:** cut sugary drinks completely, cut down refined carbs (white bread, white rice, pastries), more vegetables, legumes and protein. She was okay with time-restricted eating, but warned me not to make up for it with huge meals at the end of the window
- **Exercise:** keep running, but add 2 sessions of strength training a week. Muscle helps with glucose uptake. Also a short walk after dinner
- **Cholesterol:** LDL 162 is high. She'll re-test with the HbA1c, and if it doesn't come down with diet, we discuss statins. With possible diabetes, the LDL target is stricter
- **Liver:** ALT slightly elevated is probably mild fatty liver, which fits the picture. Ultrasound at the next visit if it's still up
- **Blood pressure:** 138/88 is borderline. She asked me to measure at home twice a day for 2 weeks and bring the log
- **Vitamin D:** 18 is low, normal for Germany in autumn. 1000 IU daily, available without prescription
- **Eyes and feet:** if the diagnosis is confirmed, I get referred to an ophthalmologist for a retina check and should get a foot exam yearly
- **Alcohol:** keep it to a minimum, which also helps the liver value

She also mentioned a structured diabetes education course covered by insurance if the diagnosis is confirmed.
