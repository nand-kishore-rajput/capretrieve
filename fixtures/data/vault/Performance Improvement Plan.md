# Performance Improvement Plan

Received the formal document on Thursday 18 September 2025 by email from Nina Vogt (HR), with Markus in CC. Officially it's called a "Structured Development Plan". Everyone knows what it is. Writing down the facts first, then my own view. Background in [[One-on-One with Manager]]. #pip #career

## The facts

- **Employee:** me, Senior Software Engineer, Billing team
- **Tenure:** 6 years and 6 months (started March 2019)
- **Plan period:** 22 September to 15 December 2025 (12 weeks)
- **Check-ins:** every two weeks with Markus and Nina. Dates: 2 Oct, 16 Oct, 30 Oct, 13 Nov, 27 Nov, 11 Dec
- **Final review:** week of 15 December
- **Possible outcomes listed in the document:** "successful completion", "extension", or "further steps in accordance with employment law"
- Signed acknowledgement requested by 26 September. I'm signing to confirm *receipt*, not agreement. I added a sentence saying so.

## The stated goals

Copied from the document, with my comments.

### Goal 1: Individual delivery

> "Complete at least 12 engineering tickets of medium complexity or higher per sprint cycle of 6 weeks, with no more than 10% rework."

- In Q1 I closed 14 tickets in a full quarter. 12 per 6 weeks is roughly double my best quarter.
- No definition of "medium complexity".
- While still running planning? Still owning two OKRs? Still on the DD project? The document doesn't say what I should drop.

### Goal 2: Collaboration and communication

> "Demonstrate constructive collaboration in team ceremonies. Peer feedback to be collected at mid-point and end of the plan."

- This is the "hard to work with" comment. Still no concrete example.
- Peer feedback "collected" by whom, from whom, with which questions? I asked. No answer yet.
- My last two 360 reviews (2023 and 2024) both rated collaboration "exceeds expectations".

### Goal 3: Ownership and accountability

> "Take clear ownership of quality issues in owned areas, including timely root cause analysis and follow-up."

- This is about the May Sev-2 again. The postmortem was done within 5 days. Every action item from it is closed.
- I wasn't the one who disabled the test.

### Goal 4: Focus and availability

> "Ensure external commitments do not affect availability during core hours (09:00 to 17:00)."

- This is about my master's program. My classes are evenings. I've never missed a core-hours meeting for it.
- This one honestly feels the most unfair. It's my personal life.

## My own assessment

Trying to be honest with myself here, not only angry.

**Where they have a point:**
- My individual coding output did drop in Q2. 6 tickets vs 14. That's real.
- I can get sharp in planning when people push for bigger commitments. I've been short with Markus in front of the team at least once (sprint 20 planning).
- I took on too much coordination without negotiating what to drop. That was my mistake too.

**Where I think it's unfair:**
- The coordination work was assigned to me. Now it's being counted against me.
- The team's numbers are the best in a year: 0.82 OKR average, commit-to-done above 0.9, zero Atlas incidents in Q3. I ran the process that produced that.
- The director chose me for a confidential, high-stakes technical review in August. You don't do that with someone who's failing.
- Goal 1 is set at a level nobody on the team has ever hit. I checked: the highest anyone reached in a 6-week window this year was 9.
- Goal 4 has nothing to do with performance.

**What it looks like to me:**
This is not a plan designed to be passed. It's a paper trail. 12 weeks ending right before the holidays, goals that are vague where they should be specific and specific where they're impossible. After 6 years, it feels like they want me out but don't want to pay for it. Possibly connected to the headcount freeze. Possibly connected to me disagreeing with leadership's integration estimate.
