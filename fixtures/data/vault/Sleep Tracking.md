# Sleep Tracking

#health #sleep

## Setup

- Tracking with my watch since 2025-03-01. It's not a lab, but it's consistent with itself, which is what I care about for comparisons
- Also a manual note every morning: bedtime, wake time, how rested I feel (1 to 5), and caffeine after 14:00 yes or no
- Target: 7.5 hours in bed, lights out by 23:15, up at 06:45 on weekdays
- Bedroom: around 18 degrees, blackout curtains since April, phone charges in the hallway (since the screen experiment, see below)

Baseline problem: I would get to bed around 00:30 after thesis work, scroll for 20 minutes, then sleep badly. Weekday average was barely over 6 hours, then I'd "catch up" on weekends and feel worse on Monday.

## Experiment 1: caffeine cutoff

**Dates:** 2025-03-17 to 2025-04-06 (3 weeks)

Before: 4 to 5 coffees a day, the last one usually around 16:30 or 17:00, plus a cola during evening study sessions maybe twice a week.

Rule: no caffeine after 13:00. That includes cola, black tea and green tea. Decaf allowed.

Results:
- Fell asleep faster: time to fall asleep went from about 25 minutes (watch estimate) to about 12 minutes
- Average sleep up roughly 40 minutes compared with baseline
- Fewer wake-ups between 03:00 and 04:00, from around 3 nights a week down to 1
- Afternoon slump around 15:00 was rough for the first 5 days, then went away
- Total coffee down to 2 or 3 a day without trying

Verdict: keeping it. I've relaxed it to a 14:00 cutoff on office days because the good espresso machine is there, and I haven't noticed a difference between 13:00 and 14:00.

## Experiment 2: screens before bed

**Dates:** 2025-04-07 to 2025-04-27 (3 weeks)

Rules:
- No phone in the bedroom. Bought a cheap 12 EUR alarm clock
- Laptop closed by 22:30, even if the thesis paragraph isn't finished
- Last 30 to 45 minutes before bed: paper book, or tidying the kitchen and prepping tomorrow's lunch
- Night shift mode on everything after 20:00 (I'm not sure this does anything)

Results:
- Bedtime moved earlier by about 30 minutes almost automatically. Without the phone there's nothing to keep me up
- Rested score up from around 3.0 to 3.4
- I read 2 novels in 3 weeks, which hasn't happened in years
- Hardest part: the urge to "just check" Slack or messages. Caught myself walking to the hallway twice the first week
- Closing the laptop at 22:30 actually made thesis sessions more focused. I stopped dragging work out late at night

Verdict: keeping the phone out of the bedroom permanently. The 22:30 laptop rule slips during deadline weeks, and the data shows it every time (see week 12).

## Running and sleep

Started the [[Running Plan]] in late April. On days with a run (always before 19:00), I fall asleep faster and the rested score averages about 0.4 higher than on rest days. Evening runs after 20:00 did the opposite, so I've stopped doing those. The two are linked in both directions: a short night before a long run means a noticeably slower pace and higher heart rate.

## Things that hurt my sleep (ranked)

1. Deadline weeks, working past 23:00
2. Alcohol, even 2 beers. Watch shows resting HR up by 6 to 8 bpm and almost no deep sleep
3. Late heavy dinners (after 21:00)
4. Caffeine after 14:00
5. Hard workouts after 20:00
6. Bedroom warmer than 20 degrees
